## How to write a ficha

Each curated record needs four prose fields:

1. **`significance`** (≈ 70 words). Why this map matters in the history of cartography or in the history of the place it depicts. State the claim plainly; avoid the word "important" — show importance.
2. **`interpretation`** (≈ 80 words). What the map reveals when read closely. Visual details: orientation, projection, what's at the centre, what's at the edges, what the colours / symbols / inscriptions do.
3. **`meaning`** (≈ 40 words). Political, cultural, or scientific stakes. One sentence on what kind of map this is — scientific, infrastructural, propagandistic, devotional, commercial, etc.
4. **`biases`** (≈ 60 words). Known errors, omissions, projections, source limitations. Be specific.

Plus the teaching notes:

5. **`teachingNotes.questions`** — three discussion questions. The first should be answerable from the record itself; the second should require interpretation; the third should be open-ended.
6. **`teachingNotes.activity`** — a single concrete classroom activity, 1–3 sentences. Should require students to do something, not just read.

## Editorial rules

- **No invention.** Every fact must come from a verifiable source (the record's `historical_context`, the linked `source_url`, or a citable secondary source). If you can't find the fact, omit it.
- **Mark uncertainty.** Use "probably", "is widely interpreted as", "remains contested" when the evidence is partial. Don't smooth over scholarly disagreement.
- **Avoid the word "important".** Show importance through specifics.
- **Stay below 250 words across the four prose fields combined.** A ficha is a teaching note, not a paper.
