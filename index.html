About Mappa Mundi

A digital museum
of maps.

Mappa Mundi exists to make the world's cartographic record legible, searchable, and contextualized — for the student writing their first essay, the teacher building a lesson, the researcher tracing a primary source, the designer mining the archive for visual ideas, and the curious traveler who simply wants to know how the place they live was once drawn.

The mission

Maps are not transparent. They are arguments — made by particular people, in particular places, for particular reasons. Mappa Mundi was built on the conviction that a map is most useful when you can see who drew it, what they were trying to do, and what they chose to leave out.

Every map in the archive is accompanied by a short essay covering its historical context, its scientific or political meaning, its known distortions, and the events surrounding its making. We treat the archive as a teaching collection, not a wallpaper library.

Sources & accuracy

The archive is built primarily on the open digital collections of the Library of Congress Geography & Map Division, which contributes the majority of the holdings, supplemented by Wikimedia Commons, Natural Earth, the USGS, the Bibliothèque nationale de France (Gallica), the Internet Archive, and a small number of other institutional contributors. All maps are distributed under open licences — public domain or Creative Commons.

Every record links back to its source institution where one is known. Essays and curated commentary exist for a small set of historically significant maps; for the rest, the archive surfaces what each source institution itself published. We label curated entries and we publish corrections when readers find them.

The archive is not neutral, and we don't pretend that it is. The selection reflects what the source institutions have digitised — which is itself an editorial position, with all the biases that implies. A map collected here is a map we consider worth thinking about, including, especially, the maps we disagree with.

Educational use

All records on Mappa Mundi are free to consult. Maps reproduced under licence from source institutions are marked accordingly; public-domain works are linked at their archival resolution on the source institution's site.

Mappa Mundi is an independent project, not affiliated with any of the source institutions. Submissions, corrections, and partnership enquiries can be addressed to the project maintainers; replace this line with your real contact details before publishing.
